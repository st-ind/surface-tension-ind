Why IB is measured in fractions of ATR, not in points
DAX volatility changes several-fold within a couple of years: ATR(14) grew from ≈190 points in 2024 to ≈300 in 2026, and the median IB went from 70 to about 110 points. Any fixed point threshold goes stale with it: the old rule "IB above 120 points — don't trade" would today reject roughly 40% of perfectly ordinary days. The ratio of IB to daily ATR, however, is stable: a median of 0.31–0.37 year after year — so that's what we lean on. For orientation at ATR ≈ 300: narrow < 75 points, normal 75–135, wide > 135, don't trade > 180.
Source: own calculation on cash DAX (Xetra 09:00–17:30), 2022–2026, 1019 days.

Trading session schedule

Three 30-minute blocks at the start of the day — periods A, B and C. The whole entry logic is built on them.

08:00–09:00
Pre-market / overnight analysis
Assess the overnight range, any gap, and the context relative to yesterday's Value Area.
09:00–09:30
Period A — read the open
The first half hour. Where did price open relative to yesterday's range? Is there a one-sided move?
09:30–10:00
Period B — the day's lean
The second IB half hour. By the end of the block it's clear which way the market leans — long or short.
10:00–10:30

IB is formed, the day's lean is clear. Three core setups: IB breakout, liquidity raid, failed auction.
10:30–15:30
Midday
Manage the open position, partial off at magnets, wait for the next setup.

Trading before the official session open — before 09:00 Berlin time. Overnight, price sets its own overnight high and low. When GER40 opens at 9:00, the market often tests those levels first — false breaks and quick reversals are common there.
03 / prep

Pre-open prep (08:00–08:59)

Three checks to run through before period A starts. Without context, entries in C are a coin flip.

🧭

Context

  • Where is price relative to yesterday's value area — above PDVAH, inside, or below PDVAL
  • All untested POCs above and below are marked — the day's magnet map
  • The main target is chosen: yesterday's POC, if it hasn't been touched yet
🌙

Overnight inventory

  • Did the night trade above or below yesterday's 17:30 close — skewed or balanced
  • ONH / ONL marked — the market checks those first
  • The gap is measured and checked against the table below
📰

Calendar

  • Ifo or flash PMI at 10:00? That lands right inside period C
  • Is today an ECB day? Then nothing after 14:00
  • US data at 14:30? The second window is in question
Overnight inventory — what it means in practice

The future trades almost around the clock; Xetra does not. While the cash market slept, a whole night of trading already happened, and by 09:00 participants are holding positions. If the entire night traded above yesterday's close, buying accumulated overnight and inventory is skewed long; entirely below — skewed short; around the close — inventory is balanced and there's no early clue.

  • With a skew, the first half hour often starts with a correction of that skew: overnight longs take profit and price gets pushed down, and vice versa
  • Opened skewed and no correction comes within 30–60 minutes → the market accepted the overnight direction: join it, don't fade it
  • The trap: a rally at the open after a night down is often just short covering, not new buying. Once the shorts are done, the rally is done. New money is confirmed by value migrating, not by the price move itself
Gaps: the size is what matters, not the fact

DAX gaps almost every single day, so the gap itself means nothing. Its size, however, tells you directly whether to expect a return to yesterday's close.

Gap sizeFills the same dayWhat to do
up to 20 points82%Almost always fills — plan for the return
20–5072%A fill candidate
50–10057%A coin flip. The gap argues neither way
100–20029%More likely won't fill. Don't wait for the return
over 20016%Trade with the gap direction or stand aside
  • Entering on a large gap — don't chase the open; wait for a failed attempt to close it: price goes to fill, tempo dies, it turns back
  • Reference point — the Xetra 17:30 close only. With the evening future (22:00) as the reference these percentages don't hold
Where the numbers come from
Own calculation on cash DAX, 2023–2026, 910 days. Independent German FDAX backtests (2005–2017) show the same picture adjusted for the lower volatility of those years — and separately confirm that the gap must be measured from the Xetra close.
Calendar: what hits DAX and when

  • ECB day → trade the morning as usual, but nothing after 14:00: the decision, the press conference and the US open merge into one continuous storm
04 / period A

Period A (09:00–09:30) — read the open

The first 30 minutes set the day's type: did price open above/below/inside yesterday's range, and did it run one-sided without pullbacks.

Open type
SituationWhat it meansAction
Above yesterday's rangeBullish contextWait for acceptance or a return inside the value area
Below yesterday's rangeBearish contextMirror image — wait for acceptance or a return inside
Inside the rangeTwo-sided auctionCareful — trade the edges, not the middle
How it moved: the four open types

The first minutes show how convinced the market is about direction. The four types run in descending order of strength: the stronger the type, the earlier you can trust the direction — and the more dangerous it is to stand against it.

TypeWhat it looks likeWhat to do
Open Drive Opens and drives one way, never trading back through the opening price. The decision was made before the open — the strongest type Never fade it. Entry — the first pullback into the A zone. Stop — beyond A's extreme. Targets: yesterday's POC → edge of yesterday's VA. The open-side extreme usually holds all day
Open Test Drive First goes to test a known level (ONL, yesterday's low), the level holds — then it turns and drives back through the open Almost as strong as a Drive. Entry — on the drive back through the opening price. Stop — beyond the test extreme: it often becomes the day's extreme
Open Rejection Reverse Moves one way weakly, gets rejected, and reverses back through the open Medium conviction, the day is more likely rotational. The reversal is tradeable, but with modest targets — this is not a trend-day thesis
Open Auction Rotates around the open with no direction Inside yesterday's range — a day without conviction: trade the edges, the open acts as a magnet. Outside the range — deceptively calm: the market may be quietly accepting new prices, a breakout can still come later
How much to trust this
The strength ladder Drive → Test Drive → Rejection → Auction is Dalton classics, proven by generations of profile traders but never publicly quantified. Use it as a confidence scale, not as probabilities. And remember: a pullback into the period A zone cancels the Drive label — the trend thesis no longer rests on it.
05 / period B

Period B (09:30–10:00) — the day's lean

By the end of the second half hour the session's lean is clear: the market is pulling long, short, or balancing. The cleaner the picture, the more confident the entries in period C.

Strength of the lean
LeanMeaningAction
StrongVWAP, periods A and B, IB — all pointing one wayTrade continuation, full size

WeakDirections disagreeWait for a cleaner setup
IB width — and what it promises

By 10:00 the Initial Balance is closed: it is the range of the first hour, periods A and B together. We measure it in fractions of the daily ATR(14) — the reasoning is in the contract overview.

IB / ATR14What it means
< 0.25Narrow. Compression → expect expansion. But careful: a narrow IB also produces more two-sided breaks — it promises movement, not a ready direction
0.25–0.45Normal (DAX median ≈ 0.35). The working IB-breakout scenario
> 0.45Wide. A meaningful part of the daily move is already spent — trim targets
> 0.6Don't trade the break — the move happened without you
  • The IB midpoint. The IB hour closed above its own midpoint → the high side gets broken noticeably more often. Below the midpoint — mirror image. The simplest early clue about which way to expect the break
  • The first extreme. The break more often runs against the first IB extreme: if the morning low printed first, the break is more likely up, and vice versa
Where the numbers come from
The IB/ATR tiers are our own DAX calculation. The midpoint and first-extreme rules were measured on ES and NQ (2015–2025, about 5500 days, two independent sources agreeing): closing above the midpoint gives a break of the high side in 75–83% of cases. The mechanics should carry over to DAX, but the exact percentages have not been measured there — treat them as orientation, not as a number.
What to look for in period C

Long lean

  • A break of the IB upper edge — the priority direction
  • A liquidity raid below with a return up — a valid entry
  • A failed break of the IB lower edge → reversal into long

Short lean

  • A break of the IB lower edge — the priority direction
  • A liquidity raid above with a return down — a valid entry
  • A failed break of the IB upper edge → reversal into short
06 / period C

Period C (10:00–10:30) — the main window

IB is formed, the day's lean is clear. Three core setups — most entries happen here.

Setup 1 · IB breakout

Normal Day
  1. IB is closed, the day's lean is moderate or strong
  2. Price broke the IB's upper or lower edge and held: for a breakout that means slow confirmation — a 30-minute period closing beyond the edge, ideally two
  3. Entry: a pullback to the IB edge — ideally with a ◆ + FVG signal in the direction of the break (see section 7)
  4. Stop: behind the nearest structure inside IB, not an arbitrary point count
  5. Check the pullback depth — the strongest post-entry filter: a first pullback of less than 25% of the IB width → continuation is close to a given; a pullback of 50% or more → the setup is dead, get out without waiting for the stop
  6. Targets: IB extensions — 0.5×, then 1× from the break edge
  7. On a trend day — target up to 1.5–2× IB
  8. Don't trade the breakout if yesterday was a neutral day
  9. A first break that price snapped straight back from is false. Don't expect a second chance from it
Where the numbers come from
Pullback depth was measured on ES and NQ, 2015–2025: with a pullback of less than a quarter of the IB width the day closes in the break direction in 94% of cases and never breaks the opposite side; with a pullback of half or more, only 25% — and every second such day becomes two-sided. Same source: the first break turns out false on roughly one day in three, and a period-C close beyond the IB edge more than doubles the odds of reaching the full 1× IB extension. These percentages are not measured on DAX — we carry over the mechanics, not the numbers.

Setup 2 · Liquidity raid

Raid
  1. Price broke the previous period's extreme and returned. Here "returned" is fast confirmation: a bar closing back beyond the level. A breakout needs time, a raid needs speed — one definition can't serve both setups
  2. Direction matches the day's lean
  3. Entry on the pullback after the return
  4. Stop: beyond the raid level
  5. Target: an untested POC → yesterday's POC → the opposite edge of the value area

Setup 3 · Failed auction

Failed Auction
  1. IB was broken but price closed back inside — the market didn't accept the level
  2. The day's lean is against the break — confirming a reversal
  3. Entry back inside IB, in the direction of the lean
  4. Target: IB midpoint → an untested POC → yesterday's POC
07 / entry confirmation

◆ + FVG — the entry trigger

Setups A–C give you context. The ◆ + FVG signal in Relief is the specific bar to enter on, once the day's picture is already in place. The alert fires on bar close.

◆ + FVG

Alert
  1. Context is ready: period C, IB closed, the day's lean is clear
  2. A period-C setup is already on the chart — ◆ + FVG confirms the entry, it doesn't replace the setup
  3. Only in the direction of the lean — Relief won't show a signal against the day's direction
  4. Entry: close of the signal bar or a pullback into the zone on the chart
  5. Stop: beyond the extreme of the ◆ bar
  6. Targets: the nearest levels of the day — POC, IB midpoint, IB extensions
  7. Price broke through the zone and held beyond it — the idea is invalidated, don't average in
08 / management

Position management

Key levels of the day for partial profit-taking, and the situations that force you out of a trade.

🔵

Untested POC

The first partial. A level from prior days that price often returns to.

🟡

VWAP

The first stop in the direction of the trade. The session's volume-weighted average price.

◐

Halfback

The midpoint of the day's or the night's range, (H+L)/2. On a trend day it is the typical pullback depth: down to halfback is normal, beyond it is a warning.

🧲

Equal highs/lows

Stop-hunt zones. A target for the second partial.

🔁

Unfinished extremes

The market often comes back to close an unfinished auction within 1–3 days.

How to exit: the full target, breakeven only behind structure

Two habits quietly eat expectancy: moving the stop to breakeven early, and taking partials "to feel calmer". Both have been backtested, and both cost money.

  • Breakeven. Don't move the stop to your entry price just "because you can". Your entry price means nothing to the market — a stop sitting there gets taken out by ordinary noise. The stop moves only once new structure appears (a new swing, a value edge), and it goes behind that
  • Partials. A trade to a magnet (untested POC, VA edge) — full exit at the target. Banking half of it midway means giving up a meaningful slice of expectancy in exchange for a prettier win rate. Keep a runner only on a trend day
  • Trailing on a trend. While the 30-minute periods keep stepping — each one higher than the last for a long, which is called one-timeframing — hold. The first period that trades below the prior period's low is the signal to take profit or tighten hard
Where the numbers come from
The cost of early breakeven stops and early partials has been measured in several independent backtests (stocks, ES) — the direction of the effect is consistent: win rate goes up, average winner falls further. The 30-minute period trail is profile doctrine with no published tests; among formally tested alternatives, a chandelier trail at 2×ATR(15m) performs best.
Forced exits
  • A liquidity raid against your position — exit immediately
  • Periods stepping against you — the second consecutive 30-minute period making a new extreme against you: exit, don't wait for the stop
  • 3+ consecutive balancing periods — the move has run out of steam, no point holding
09 / second window

Second window (15:30–17:00) — a new auction

15:30 is not a "continuation of the morning" but a new auction: the Americans open their session and re-price everything Europe traded before them.

It helps to flip the picture: our morning is to the Americans what the night is to us. It is their overnight inventory. If Europe trended up, the US open either accelerates that trend or starts by correcting the accumulated skew — exactly the same logic as the overnight inventory in the morning.

  • Reset at 15:25. Re-read the day from scratch: where price sits relative to IB, VWAP and the value area. The first 15–30 minutes of the US open form their own mini-IB — trade its edges
  • Why the window is alive. Full IB extensions are most often reached in the afternoon, together with American volume — entering a continuation of the day's trend here is statistically sound, not "chasing a departed train"
  • The best entry is a ◆ + FVG signal in the direction of the day's lean, once context is already in place
  • Careful: the 14:30 data (CPI, NFP) lands an hour before the window and can invalidate the entire morning structure. On ECB days skip the window entirely
  • Stops tighter than usual — less time left in the day for the trade to work
Where the numbers come from
Extension timing was measured on ES and NQ: only a small share of full IB extensions is reached by midday, the bulk arrives in the afternoon. DAX hourly statistics support this indirectly: the average ranges of the 15:00–17:00 hours nearly match the first hour of the session, while 12:00–14:00 is a dead zone at roughly 60% of it. Reading the day as two auctions is the practice of European profile traders.
10 / day types

Day types and tactics

The day type becomes clear by mid-session and sets the tactics through the close.

The main thing to know before any table: roughly 7 days out of 10 rotate in both directions, and a genuine trend day is about 1 in 10. So the default posture is trading from the edges toward the middle, while betting on a breakout and continuation is the exception that has to prove itself: a Drive open, a narrow IB, periods stepping.

Signs

Narrow IB + one-sided open + a strong lean

Tactics

Normal

Tactics

Neutral

Neutral Day

Signs

Tactics

ORR

Open-Rejection-Reverse

Signs

Tactics

Enter on the retest of the VA edge from inside, target the PDPOC

⚠️ The main mistake on a trend day

  • Fading the staircase. If the 30-minute periods are one-timeframing — each new one higher (or lower) than the last — do not enter against that staircase at all, however "overbought" the bars look. Trend days are rare, but they are exactly where edge-traders lose the most.
A DAX quirk
DAX trends noticeably more than the US indices: it closes inside the IB on only 29% of days, and extends 1× IB or more on over half of them. But it also produces more two-sided breaks (about 39% of days), so false pokes are plentiful. Conclusion: breakouts do work on DAX, but only together with the pullback-depth filter from the IB breakout setup.
Source: own calculation on cash DAX, 2025–2026.
11 / risk

Risk and position size

Structure gives the stop first, the stop gives the size second. Never the other way round.

Risk per trade

The order is always the same: structure → stop → size. The stop doesn't move so the size fits, and the size doesn't grow because the setup "looks really good". The arithmetic: contracts = euro risk ÷ (stop in points × point value), with risk per trade at 0.5–1% of the account.

ContractPoint valueRisk on a 40-pt stopAccount that carries it
FDAX€25 (tick 0.5 = €12.50)€1000from ~€100–200k
FDXM (mini)€5€200from ~€20–40k
FDXS (micro)€1€40from ~€4–8k
  • Micros are nothing to be ashamed of. 5 FDXS = 1 FDXM. On a smaller account a partial exit is mathematically possible only with micros: you can't close one contract in pieces
The daily stop — the best-evidenced rule on this page

🛑 −2R on the day, or 3 stops in a row → the day is over

  • No exceptions, no "I'll win it back now". The terminal gets closed, physically
  • Calibration: hitting the daily stop more than twice a month means the problem isn't the market, it's the size. Cut it in half
  • Journal: grade every trade A/B/C on checklist adherence, not on the result. A profitable trade taken outside the rules is a C. Trust a setup's statistics after ~100 trades — before that you simply cannot tell edge from noise
Why this rule outranks the others
In a study of more than 500,000 prop-trader accounts, 71% of failures were daily-loss-limit breaches — not bad setups, not the market. "Failing to call it a day" is the single most common behavioural issue: among traders it affects, the share who end up profitable drops roughly threefold. The "more than twice a month — cut size" rule is SMB Capital prop-desk practice. The 100-trade threshold is ordinary statistics: at 50 trades the 95% confidence interval on win rate is ±14 percentage points, at 100 it is ±10. Everything else on this page only works together with this section.
12 / stop list

When NOT to trade

⚠️ Red flags

  • Weak lean, an Auction open inside the range — there's no narrative for the day, nothing to enter on
  • Yesterday was a neutral day + today opened inside the range — a trap at the IB edge
  • A release inside your entry window — Ifo or PMI at 10:00, US data at 14:30, an ECB day: the move is driven by the event, not the auction
  • POC sitting right at the IB edge — the move there is magnetic, not tradable
  • IB > 0.6 × ATR14 — the trend already started without you, the moment is gone
  • One-timeframing against your idea — periods stepping against the direction of the trade
  • The daily limit is spent — the day is over, see section 11
