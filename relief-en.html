Signals Marks and levels

Every mark shows up right on the chart — no separate panel.

▲↑ bullish ▼↓ bearish ◆ inside – neutral OC

Period marks

The first symbol is range direction (H/L vs. the previous period). The second is open→close direction. An uninterrupted run of ▲ or ▼ feeds into HL Sweep.

Raid G ↑

Grab — a quick touch

Price pushed past the previous period's extreme and closed back inside. Stops are swept, a reversal is likely. Confirmed or removed after N candles.

Raid S ↑

Sweep — holding past the level

Price closed past the extreme or spent N bars beyond it. A strong grab with follow-through. Continuation is more likely.

▲ L 4/5

Bias — a scored session lean

On period B — a ▲ L 1–5/5 or ▼ S 1–5/5 score: VWAP, A and B direction, an IBH/IBL break, position versus IB Mid. A standalone chart mark — context for an entry, not a filter on other signals.

▲×3 ↑

HL Sweep — a run gets taken out

After N periods in a row of ▲ or ▼, the next period breaks the last extreme and snaps back quickly. The number is the run length — a sign of trend exhaustion.

Outside

Day open type

Outside (above) — opened above pVAH, a bullish context. Outside (below) — below pVAL, a bearish context. Inside (danger) — inside the Value Area, a two-sided auction.

🧲 EQH

Equal Highs / Lows + Magnet

Levels touched repeatedly without breaking — stops stacked behind them. A cluster of N such levels within X points gets marked 🧲 — a strong pull zone.

OD↑ / OD↓

Open Drive — a trending start

Period A has zero TPO blocks against the move's direction. OD↑ — bullish blocks only, OD↓ — bearish only. The strongest sign of a trend day from the first few minutes.

IB 1×

IB extensions

Once price clears the Initial Balance, the 0.5×, 1×, 1.5×, and 2× levels become reaction zones. A break past IB × the threshold by session end → Trend Day.

IBm

IB Mid — level test

A marker on the first touch of the IB midpoint in period C+. The main magnet on a balancing day — appears right after period B closes.

CHoCH↓

Change of Character

A break of a swing extreme in the direction opposite the previous break — the first sign of a trend change. Unlike BOS (a break with the previous direction), CHoCH catches the actual structural reversal.

BOS↑

Break of Structure

A break of the previous swing high (BOS↑) or swing low (BOS↓) in the direction of the previous break — trend continuation. The mark appears on the breaking bar. Swing sensitivity is configurable, from short 1m waves up to large structures.

Trading day 7 steps · from open to entry

The analysis sequence for a trading day, in order.

Step 01

Before the open: context

pVAH / pVAL — where was yesterday's Value Area? Are there untested nPOCs above/below? These are magnets for today's move.

Step 02

Period A: open type

Outside above/below — a trend-day context. Inside (danger) — a two-sided auction. Check Open Drive and the open type in the info panel.

Step 03

Period B: Bias

▲ L n/5 or ▼ S n/5 — a score from VWAP, A/B, IB, and IB Mid. Trade with the bias unless there's a strong opposing setup.

Step 04

VWAP as a filter

Above VWAP — longs, below — shorts. The first test (★) matters most. Circles mark weaker wick tests.

Step 05

Raid / HL Sweep

Raid G/S and ▲×N/▼×N — liquidity grabs after a run of periods. The arrow direction is the expected move once confirmed.

Step 06

BOS / CHoCH: confirm structure

After a Raid — check swing structure. BOS with the bias confirms continuation, CHoCH signals a trend change earlier than anything else. Combine with Bias and open type.

Step 07

Targets: Poor H/L, nPOC, EQH

Poor H/L, Single Print, nPOC, and EQH/EQL — zones price gravitates toward. Use them as take-profits or reversal points.
